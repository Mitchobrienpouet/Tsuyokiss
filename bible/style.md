# English translation style

Target natural, energetic English comedy with precise source meaning. Takahiro's
dialogue relies on rapid banter, affectionate insults, reversals, parody, and
sharp register changes; preserve the last-beat punchline instead of explaining
it. Leo's first-person narration is casual, observant, and self-deprecating.

- Keep Japanese name order only in source notes; English prose uses given name
  naturally. Locked romanizations remain unchanged.
- Keep honorifics only when characterization or hierarchy materially depends on
  them; otherwise use natural English address.
- Preserve `「...」` around spoken dialogue. Narration remains unquoted.
- Use ASCII `...`, `--`, straight apostrophes, and ordinary spaces. Do not use
  smart quotes, Unicode ellipses, or em/en dashes.
- Preserve deliberate censored text such as `XX`; do not reconstruct it.
- Render puns and pop-culture parody with an English joke of the same function,
  but never add a claim or reference absent from source/context.
- Do not flatten recurring insults and nicknames. Use the locks in glossary.md.
- Preserve ambiguity, reveal timing, tense/aspect, speaker, and who does what to
  whom. Every material English proposition must be grounded in Japanese.
- Do not insert engine codes. Translate only the displayed body text.

Source research: VNDB v397; CandySoft's official Tsuyokiss world notes; Japanese
Wikipedia; Japanese strategy/character wikis. Any uncertain romanization must
be logged as contested rather than silently invented.
